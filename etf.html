How the numbers are calculated

Break-even fee. The ETF is shorted at the first close of the window and held to the latest close, using Yahoo's dividend-adjusted (total return) prices. Borrow is charged daily on the current market value of the short, on a 360-day year, so the short's profit as a share of its starting size is −ETF total return − fee × A, where A is the sum over days of the short's value relative to its starting value × days ÷ 360. Solving for the fee gives:

  • Short profits: fee = −ETF return ÷ A
  • Short beats SPY: fee = (−ETF return − SPY return) ÷ A, the fee at which the short earned the same as owning SPY over the same dates.
  • Hedged pair (in each ETF's details): short the ETF and buy SPY with the same dollars, fee = (SPY return − ETF return) ÷ A.

Worthwhile means the quoted fee is below the beat-SPY break-even. Interest earned on short proceeds, margin costs and the risk of recalls or fee spikes are left out.

Theoretical fee. Each holding's weight in the ETF's latest report × that holding's IBKR borrow fee, summed. Holdings are matched by ISIN (or ticker); anything without an IBKR fee, such as cash, Treasuries or swaps, is charged the "fee for unmatched holdings" above. Swap and futures exposure isn't converted into its underlying stock, so for leveraged ETFs that hold mostly cash and swaps the theoretical fee sits close to that assumed rate.

Sources. Quoted fees, rebates and availability: Interactive Brokers' public stock loan file, refreshed about every 15 minutes. Prices and history: Yahoo Finance. Holdings: the ETF's latest SEC N-PORT filing (complete, but filed quarterly and published up to 60 days later), or Yahoo's top 10 holdings when no filing is found.
